The rule. A release that gives one wrong answer in this test does not ship. Saying “I couldn't confirm that” is allowed; guessing is not. Questions about you (“what's my address”) are never sent anywhere, and the test checks that too.

How it is measured

358 everyday questions, the kind people of every age ask: capitals, authors, dates, science, prices, who runs what, personal questions, and trick questions built to catch a wrong answer. Each is graded against the known right answer. A figure that changes, like a price, is graded on the right topic, place, unit and a believable range. An officeholder or a CEO is graded only against what the news confirms today, never against memory: the test caught its own answer key out of date when Nvidia's CEO changed on November 12.

What “right” does not claim: a test this size cannot prove Eve is never wrong. Zero wrong in 229 answers says the error rate is very likely under about 2%. The test grows with every release, and every wrong answer anyone reports becomes a new question in it.
