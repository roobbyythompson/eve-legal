Eve — Privacy Policy

The short version: your private companion journey stays on your iPhone unless you explicitly protect it. Your EVE social account is separate and can be recovered with Sign in with Apple.

Last updated: November 4, 2026

Eve doesn’t sell your data, doesn’t show ads, and doesn’t track you across other apps or websites. Your private mood history, check-in notes, companion conversations, and durable memories stay on your device. Only content you explicitly choose to share enters a social audience.

1. Where your data lives

Your mood and energy logs, private companion conversations, Eve’s memories, goals, and private wellness notes are stored locally on your iPhone by default. Public profile fields, relationships, circles, posts, social media, and encrypted messaging delivery use Eve’s Supabase social service so the people and devices you selected can receive them. Optional private journey backup, Health, Calendar, weather, Feed Agents, and other connections are described below.

2. AI choices

Eve’s companion relationship layer, safety routing, memory, and device-action approvals operate on your iPhone without requiring a separate model download. On supported devices, Eve may use Apple’s on-device intelligence for language generation. You can also choose an optional compatible model from Eve’s private model library; once downloaded, that model runs on your iPhone.

Frontier intelligence and live web is not offered for purchase to ordinary users in Eve 1.0. If an authorized account explicitly enables that separate route, Eve sends the current, explicit web-search question and nothing else through Eve’s authenticated Supabase Edge Function to OpenAI. Eve does not include recent turns, identity, name, handle, profile, purpose, strengths, goals, memories, Health data, Calendar events, faith or cultural notes, social feed, or location with the request. A pseudonymous safety identifier may accompany it, and Eve requests that the model API not store the response.

If you choose to download an optional model, your iPhone downloads those files directly from the third-party host that publishes them. That host can see your device’s network request for the files, the same as any download. Your conversations, memories, and personal context are never sent there. Chat scrollback and Eve’s growth memories remain stored locally, and you can clear the conversation or delete memories at any time.

Eve also uses Apple’s on-device NaturalLanguage framework to help recognize language that may indicate you’re in distress, so it can point you to real support resources. This analysis runs entirely on your device before any model route and is not stored separately from the message itself.

3. Looking things up

The globe above the keyboard decides whether Eve may look outside your iPhone. It is on when you install the app, and you can switch it off at any time. With it off, Eve answers from what is already on your device and says plainly that it did not look anything up.

Only a question about the world can leave your phone. Anything about you — how you feel, your people, your body, your relationships, or anything else phrased as yours — stays on the device whatever this setting says. Eve declines the lookup rather than sending it.

When a world question does leave, it goes straight from your iPhone to public sources, never through an Eve server and never to an AI service. Eve reads the question on your phone and sends only its public part: for “how tall is Mount Everest”, the words “Mount Everest”. These requests carry no account identifier, name or handle; like any request on the internet, they reach the source with your device’s network address.

Sources that may receive the public words of a question: Wikipedia, Wikidata and Wiktionary (encyclopedia summaries, structured facts and definitions); the World Bank (a country’s population and area, asked for by its two-letter country code); NHTSA (vehicle recalls, asked for by make, model and year — never a VIN); RxNorm, openFDA and MedlinePlus (what a medicine’s public label says it is for, by the medicine’s name; and the newest food recall); PubChem (chemical formulas and molecular weights, by the compound’s name); ChEMBL (a second record of the same formula, by the compound’s name); the NIST National Vulnerability Database (a security bulletin, by its CVE id); the RCSB Protein Data Bank (a protein structure, by its id); NCBI Gene and Ensembl (a human gene, by its symbol); UniProt, InterPro and AlphaFold (a protein, by its accession); The Metropolitan Museum of Art (whether it holds a work, by the work’s title); NASA JPL (asteroid close approaches, by a date window and an asteroid designation); NASA Exoplanet Archive (how many confirmed planets a named star has); ITIS and GBIF (an animal’s or plant’s scientific name, by its common name), with WoRMS, OBIS, NCBI Taxonomy and ENA checking that same scientific name; Europe PMC, DOAJ, Zenodo, the Library of Congress, ROR, legislation.gov.uk and ClinicalTrials.gov (papers, articles, datasets, collections, research organizations, UK Acts and clinical-trial counts, by the subject you name); Nominatim from OpenStreetMap (where a place is, by its name), with the USGS National Map and NASA POWER (its elevation and its usual temperature, by its coordinates); the Art Institute of Chicago, the Cleveland Museum of Art and the Victoria and Albert Museum (who made an artwork, by its title); Datamuse (rhymes, by the word); Our World in Data (life expectancy, by country); the European Central Bank (exchange rates when Frankfurter is down, by a currency code); Frankfurter (currency exchange rates from the European Central Bank, by two currency codes); the NASA Image and Video Library (NASA pictures, by the subject you name); Google News (news search, with your device’s region and language); Open Library, Internet Archive, MusicBrainz, Crossref, OpenAlex and PubMed (books, archives, music, research and medical research); the Federal Register, National Archives, FBI Vault, CIA Reading Room, ODNI, AARO and the US Department of Justice (public government records); and the Apple Music catalog, when you search for a song or ask who sings one, by its title.

Sources Eve reads without sending anything of yours: public feeds and pages from BBC, NPR, The New York Times, Dow Jones, The Guardian, ESPN, NASA, Space.com, Smithsonian, ScienceDaily, Carbon Brief, Rolling Stone, Billboard, Pitchfork, Apple Music charts and Megaphone podcast feeds; the US Geological Survey’s volcano and earthquake feeds (USGS Volcano Hazards Program and USGS Earthquake Hazards Program) and NASA’s natural-event tracker (NASA EONET), read to answer questions about volcanoes, earthquakes, wildfires and storms; the NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center (the Sun’s latest flare and the northern-lights index) and NASA JPL’s fireball record, read to answer questions about the sky; the US Treasury’s Debt to the Penny record, MLB and NHL standings, and NIST’s table of physical constants; the NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory, NASA GISS, NOAA NCEI, the National Hurricane Center, the Environment Agency, NOAA Tides and Currents and USGS Water Services (carbon dioxide, the global temperature record, US monthly temperatures, active storms, England’s flood warnings, tides and river flows at well-known stations); BLS (the consumer price index); and the US Naval Observatory (the moon’s phases); public podcast transcripts on Podscripts, read to sum up in one line what a guest argued; and public video previews from YouTube, Vimeo and Dailymotion. Publisher feeds you chose in Feed Agents are fetched as described in the Feed Agents section.

Weather sends a place to Open-Meteo, as described in the location section. Asking about weather alerts or disaster declarations in a US state sends that state’s two-letter code to the National Weather Service or FEMA — never your own location. The optional on-device model is downloaded from Hugging Face only if you choose to install it; the download sends nothing about you, and the model runs entirely on your iPhone.

Eve does not send your name, handle, profile, memories, private companion conversations, mood or charge history, Health data, Calendar events, photos, messages, or contacts with any of these lookups.

4. Health data

If you connect Apple Health, Eve reads activity data (steps, energy, exercise, sleep, water, mindful minutes, workouts) only with your permission, to show your fitness rings and personal context. Eve never writes to Apple Health — it does not create, edit, or delete entries in your health record. Health data never leaves your device, is never shared with anyone, is never used for advertising, and is never sold — period, and this applies even if you later disconnect Health or delete Eve.

5. Calendar

If you connect Calendar, Eve reads plain event titles and start times for the next 48 hours only, to give the companion sensible timing for check-ins and to mention what’s coming up. Eve never reads event notes, locations, or attendees. When you explicitly ask Eve to schedule something, it creates a local draft containing only the event title and proposed time and opens Apple’s Calendar editor. Nothing is added until you review the details and tap Add; Eve does not silently edit or delete existing events. Calendar data stays on your device and is used only inside your own chat context.

6. Location and weather

If you enable automatic atmosphere and allow location access, Eve requests an approximate location (about kilometer-level accuracy). Latitude and longitude are sent to Open-Meteo to return current weather, and Apple location services may provide a nearby city label. Eve does not use this information for advertising or tracking. You can turn automatic atmosphere off and deny location access at any time in iOS Settings.

7. Apple Music

Apple Music is the only music service Eve connects to. With your permission Eve searches the Apple Music catalog so you can pin a song to your profile or attach one to a post. Eve stores only the song’s public catalog details — title, artist, artwork, catalog id, and Apple’s thirty-second preview address. Eve does not read your library, your listening history, or your Apple Music account, and it never streams audio itself. Eve’s separate on-device vibe catalog contains metadata only and does not contain or play audio.

8. EVE accounts, Feed Agents, and Supabase

Eve uses Supabase Auth and its social service for account identifiers, public names and handles, profile fields you choose to publish, relationships and circles, posts, encrypted message delivery, moderation, and rate limits. Before Sign in with Apple, Eve may use a passwordless anonymous Supabase Auth identifier.

When you use Sign in with Apple, Apple provides a stable account identifier and may provide an Apple private-relay email address. Supabase stores that authentication information linked to your EVE account. Eve does not display the relay email on your profile or use it for advertising.

Supabase Auth and API infrastructure also records the IP address and user agent attached to authentication and service requests in security and audit logs, where they can be associated with the account. Eve uses these logs only for authentication security, abuse prevention, and reliability — never advertising or cross-app tracking.

Feed Agents send only the catalog keys for agents you selected to retrieve public publisher content. Your custom agent names, engagement learning, private companion chat, memories, notes, Health, Calendar, media, and location are not sent with Feed Agent requests. Sharing an item uses Apple’s share sheet with the publisher’s canonical link and no EVE personalization data attached.

If you allow notifications, Eve sends this iPhone’s push token to that same service so it can deliver alerts. The token is not used for advertising. Alert choices you make in chat, such as quieting likes, stay on this iPhone.

Clips uses YouTube API Services. To fill Clips, your iPhone and Eve's service request public YouTube channel feeds and video details. YouTube receives those requests and the device or server IP address, never your EVE account, chats, memories or other personal data. YouTube videos play in YouTube's own player, which Google operates. By watching YouTube videos in Eve you agree to the YouTube Terms of Service (https://www.youtube.com/t/terms); Google's Privacy Policy (https://policies.google.com/privacy) describes how Google handles data from its player. What you watch and skip in Clips stays on this iPhone and shapes Clips there.

If you send a problem report — Report a Problem in Settings, or by shaking your iPhone — Eve's service stores your words, the category and steps you chose to add, any technical details you approved, and your account's @handle when you have one, then emails them with the report's reference number to EVE's support inbox so the problem can be fixed and followed up. A report from an account with no @handle says so rather than naming anyone. Deleting your account detaches your reports from it, including the @handle.

9. How long social content stays

Stories last 24 hours, then they are gone.

Clips play for 90 days. After that the whole clip post is gone — the video and the caption. You can always post a new clip. Your three most recent clips stay on your profile; posting a fourth retires the oldest one entirely.

Photos you post stay. You can keep up to 15 live photos on your public profile.

A chat or group chat holds 500 messages. At 500, each person clears the whole chat on their iPhone, kept messages too, to keep talking. A chat disappears after 48 hours unless both people choose Keep in that conversation. Either person can switch back to 48 hours. Photos and videos in a 48-hour chat open once, then vanish for both of you.

10. Friend and group messages

Eve supports live direct and group messaging. Message text, captions, photos, and supported short videos are encrypted on the sending iPhone before upload. The delivery service and private media bucket store ciphertext rather than readable content.

The service stores public device-key packages, conversation membership, sender and recipient identifiers, content type, timestamps, expiration, and delivery/read receipts to route messages. Those routing records are metadata and are not end-to-end encrypted. A newly added or recovered device can send and receive new messages after registering its key, but it may not decrypt messages or media encrypted before that device key existed. Eve cannot prevent a recipient from saving, recording, or screenshotting content.

11. Companion plans between friends

Eve's companion can carry a plan to a friend's companion — “ask Sam if he wants to get coffee Friday”. All of it is off until you turn it on.

Nothing is sent without you. Eve drafts the plan, reads it back, and sends it only after you say yes in that same turn. Silence, a question, or a change of subject are not consent. The activity and any note are text you typed; the companion never writes them for you.

How it travels. A plan is an ordinary message in the encrypted conversation you already have with that person, encrypted on your iPhone before upload exactly like a photo. Our service learns only what it learns about a photo — that a conversation had traffic. There is no separate plans database, so we never hold a record of who is planning what with whom.

Both people decide, and the stricter setting wins. Your companion may coordinate only with people you are mutually tapped in with, unless you widen it to shared circles. Your setting and theirs must both allow it; neither of you can widen the other. Your default is mutuals only.

Who can see a plan. A plan is private to the two of you by default. When you confirm one, you can choose Private, Friends, or Public. The other person's choice can only narrow that. Friends means people you have both tapped in with, on the companion's profile. Public means anyone who can already open that companion profile. A public plan is not a post on the public feed, and choosing it does not publish the app. Nothing is listed before the other person has answered. Agreeing on a plan does not spend a move, and a companion does not invent a plan on its own.

When your companion speaks first. If you allow it, your companion may notice that you and a mutual friend have not spoken in a while and ask whether you would like it to reach out. This is off by default. It reads only counts, direction, and timestamps — never the contents of any message, which it could not read in any case. It is limited to at most three such messages a day across everyone, respects your quiet hours, and stays quiet about people you have barely spoken to. Your companion never speaks to your friend without your yes.

What we still refuse, at every setting. Your companion will not publish invented conversations between itself and another person's companion. Only something a person actually typed and confirmed travels.

12. Optional account and journey recovery

Recovery has two separate layers. First, Sign in with Apple authenticates your permanent EVE account through Supabase. On another supported iPhone signed into the same Apple account, that can restore your public profile, handle, social graph, feed access, and the ability to use a fresh companion and send new messages even when iCloud Drive or iCloud Keychain is turned off. Native Sign in with Apple uses the Apple account available on that device; it is not a password field for an arbitrary Apple account on an unsigned-in device.

Second, if you choose Protect private journey with iCloud, Eve encrypts your core journey on your iPhone with AES-GCM before saving ciphertext in your private iCloud Drive, while a random key is synchronized through iCloud Keychain. This includes profile, check-ins, companion chat, memories, progress, learned support preferences, and saved mission data that already exists on the device and that 1.0 does not show. Photos, videos, social DMs, and historical message device keys are not included.

If iCloud Drive or Keychain is unavailable during Apple account recovery, Eve keeps the social account usable but marks the old private journey as waiting for iCloud. It does not generate a replacement key or upload a fresh backup over the protected journey.

13. What we don’t do

We don’t sell your data. We don’t show ads. We don’t use App Tracking Transparency because we don’t track you across other apps or websites, and we don’t share your data with data brokers or advertising networks. Your private mood history, check-in notes, and durable memories stay on your device. Private companion chat stays on-device unless you explicitly enable Frontier, in which case only the bounded request described above leaves the device. Only content you explicitly choose to share enters a social audience. Words you use to tune your feed, such as asking for more of a topic, stay on this iPhone and are not sent with lookups or Feed Agent requests.

14. What others in the app can see

Your public profile link can show your name, handle, join date, and public profile fields. New accounts are private by default. Public-account posts can be viewed by other EVE users; private-account posts require the relationship level shown in the app. Check-ins stay private by default. If you explicitly share vibe and charge with circles, joined circles receive only that coarse signal. You separately control charge, friends, replies, AI-companion links, and media visibility.

A post for your Tap Ins is stored by Eve's service and shown only to you and the people you are mutually tapped in with. A post in a circle stays in that circle: only its members see it, with its photos, videos, comments and likes. Neither is copied onto the public feed or into hashtag discovery.

Before a post, story, profile photo or banner leaves your iPhone, Eve checks the picture on the device, in every audience. It uses Apple's Sensitive Content Analysis when you have turned on Sensitive Content Warning in iPhone Settings, plus Apple's on-device image and text recognition. Pictures are not sent to a moderation company. A picture the check stops does not post. Eve does not allow nudity or sexual content anywhere: the feed, Tap Ins, circles, stories or profiles. Each stopped attempt to post nudity is a strike on your account, which Eve's service keeps with its time and the audience you chose, at most one every two minutes. At three strikes, posting, replies and stories are paused until EVE reviews the account, your posts are hidden from other people's feeds, and EVE's support inbox receives an email with the account's handle and display name. On-device checks are not perfect, and Apple's layer works only when you turn it on, so EVE also relies on people reporting and blocking, as the Terms describe.

You can hide or report a public post from its menu. Hiding removes it from your feed on this iPhone. A report follows the moderation path in the app.

When your account sees someone else's public post, Eve's service records that view once, so the author can see how many people saw it. Authors see the number, never who.

15. Purchases

Eve 1.0 does not sell anything. The Amplify sheet may name Spark, Flux, Eve+, and Frontier. Checkout is locked, so none of those can be bought, and Frontier is not a live route. Amplify Studio is locked for now. Planning with a friend, your private companion, the feed, circles, and messages do not require a purchase. If a later version sells a move through Apple In-App Purchase, this policy will be updated before that sale. Eve will not receive your card number or billing address from Apple.

16. Deleting your data

Settings → Account → Delete account first requests deletion of the authenticated Supabase account and its EVE social data, then permanently erases the local journey and media. If cloud deletion cannot be confirmed, Eve leaves local credentials and data intact so you can retry. If private journey recovery was enabled, Eve also removes the encrypted iCloud backup and recovery credentials. Deleting the app without this option may leave a cloud account or encrypted iCloud backup in place. Deleting your account also removes your posts, comments, stories, strike records, and the photos and videos you sent in chats.

17. Children

Eve is for people 18 and older. We don't knowingly collect personal information from anyone under 18. If we learn that someone under 18 has used Eve, we will delete the account. Parents who believe their child has used Eve can contact us below.

18. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct, export, or delete your personal data, and to object to or restrict certain processing. Because almost everything Eve stores lives only on your device, you can exercise most of these rights directly, at any time, through Settings — viewing your data in the Brain tab, editing it in Edit Profile, or erasing it with Delete account. For anything else, contact us at the address below.

19. Changes

If this policy changes in a way that affects your data, the app will tell you. The latest version lives in Settings → Legal and at this public URL.
